THE QUESTION

What can a first order tell us?

Measure how often customers purchase again within 90 days, then test whether their first order helps predict that outcome.

Dataset: Chen, D. (2015), UCI Online Retail, December 2010 to December 2011. Data and derived results: CC BY 4.0.

THE WORKFLOW

  1. PrepareClean product-level transactions in Python. Keep identified customers with positive quantities and prices.
  2. StructureAggregate invoices, sequence orders, and label customers with a complete 90-day observation window. The repository includes Snowflake SQL and a local Python reproduction.
  3. EvaluateTrain a scaled logistic regression on first-order value, quantity, and distinct products. Compare with a majority-class baseline.

THE RESULT THAT MATTERS

A useful analysis.
A limited predictor.

The model only slightly outperformed the baseline. This logistic regression, using three first-order features, showed weak discrimination on the held-out test set. This version is not deployment-ready.

How the 90-day window was defined Methodology

Each customer’s first observed order starts their own 90-day window. Customers are included only when the dataset continues for at least 90 days after that order.

Customers who do not return remain in the denominator. Customers without enough follow-up are excluded rather than labeled as non-returners. “First observed” is important because earlier purchases may fall outside this dataset.

What the model got right and missed Evaluation

Most returners were missed.

Of 321 actual returners in the test set, the model identified 58. That is about 18.1% recall at a classification threshold of 0.5.

Accuracy alone hides this weakness. A useful next experiment would test additional information available at prediction time and use a time-based evaluation with fully observed outcomes.

These are proposed next steps, not completed improvements.

How a business could use this analysis Interpretation

The observed repeat-purchase rate provides a reference for discussing retention. Repeating the same definition across comparable cohorts could help a business investigate whether customer behavior is changing.

The model is an exploratory test, not a customer-targeting recommendation. This project does not establish marketing uplift, causation, or how well the model would perform on future customers.
